# Credits

- The lower bound gap >= c / (n^3 C_PI) for coordinate hit-and-run on convex bodies containing a unit ball, and the statement that this rate is conjectured to be asymptotically tight: Y. Kook and S. S. Vempala, arXiv:2608.16878, Theorem 1.1 and Section 1 [KV]. Used in the introduction and in the Theta(n^2), Theta(n^-5) corollary.
- Coordinate hit-and-run as the Gibbs sampler: V. F. Turchin (1971) [Turchin].
- Polynomial mixing of coordinate hit-and-run, and a body on which its conductance is worse than that of hit-and-run and the ball walk: A. Laddha and S. Vempala, Discrete Comput. Geom. 70 (2023) [LV20].
- Warm-start mixing bounds between the l_infinity ball and its dilation: H. Narayanan and P. Srivastava, Combin. Probab. Comput. 31 (2022) [NS20].
- l_0 isoperimetry for convex bodies and the resulting coordinate hit-and-run bounds: M. Fernandez, arXiv:2608.27854 [Fernandez].
- Hit-and-run mixing in terms of the outer radius: L. Lovász and S. Vempala, SIAM J. Comput. 35 (2006) [LV06].
- The Poincaré constant upper bound uses the diameter inequality C_PI <= diam^2 / pi^2 for convex domains of L. E. Payne and H. F. Weinberger, Arch. Rational Mech. Anal. 5 (1960) [PW], in the form of L. Esposito, C. Nitsch and C. Trombetti, arXiv:1110.2960, Theorem 1.1 at p = 2 [ENT].

New in this paper: the polytopes K_n, the bounds on C_PI and on the coordinate hit-and-run gap for K_n, and the conclusion that the factor n^3 in the Kook-Vempala bound is sharp.
